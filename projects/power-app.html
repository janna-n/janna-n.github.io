Overview

Self-taught Power Apps and delivered a functional workforce check-in prototype in three days, connecting digital attendance tracking with an Excel-based reporting workflow.

Problem

As construction was wrapping up, our company was preparing to hand over the responsibilities to the new maintenance team. One of the things they needed was a simple way to record daily workforce attendance to generate a Daily Force Report, but they didn’t yet have a digital system of their own.

During a conversation with senior management about tools needed for the transition, Power Apps came up as a possible solution. Although I had used the company’s existing app as an end user, I had never worked with Power Apps or built an application before.

With only three days left on site before the end of my contract, I decided to see whether I could build a working prototype from scratch.

My Role

Working independently, I taught myself the fundamentals of Power Apps in just three days. Additionally, I adapted an existing Excel reporting process into a simplified system for our maintenance team.

The goal here wasn't to build a polished production application. The goal was to deliver a functional prototype that could support the handover and give the team a working solution while a permanent system was being developed.

Solution

I built a Power App that connected to an Excel workbook stored on OneDrive, which allowed attendance information entered through the app to update the reporting spreadsheet in real time.

The prototype included:

  • Searchable employee lookup using partial name matching.
  • Attendance options for “Here”, “Absent”, and “Day Off”.
  • Automatic overwriting of previous entries so attendance could easily be corrected without needing to update the spreadsheet manually.
  • Date selection from the Excel workbook.

A simplified version of our Excel DFR reporting workbook, which better meets the needs of maintenance.

The most challenging part of this project was learning Power Apps. Since I had no prior development experience, I had to work through unfamiliar formulas and data connections while figuring out how the platform handled user input, searching, and data updates.

Despite the learning curve, I still managed to deliver a working prototype before leaving the project.

Result

The completed prototype was demonstrated to management, who approved it as it provided the maintenance team with an interim solution during the transition from construction to operations.

Although it was intentionally built as a rapid prototype rather than a finished product, it still successfully demonstrated that attendance could be captured digitally and that reporting data could be updated automatically via an Excel backend.

This project demonstrates my ability to learn an unfamiliar platform under pressure and deliver a practical solution within an extremely short timeframe.
