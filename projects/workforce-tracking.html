Overview

Built a centralized Excel-based workforce management system that transformed a manual tracking process into a real-time operational tool supporting nearly 1,000 employees, multiple shifts, and several departments.

Problem

When I came aboard this project, there was no reliable way to track the nearly 1,000 workers across multiple shifts, forepersons (FM), general forepersons (GMs), and work areas.

The primary tracking system in place was a single physical whiteboard. However, since management was so focused on running the project, it was rarely up to date. Finding an employee on site often meant asking over the radio who they worked for. Balancing crews was difficult, shift planning was inconsistent, and information was scattered across departments and not communicated.

The project managers recognized that keeping track of the workforce had become a full-time job, so they created a dedicated role for me to help bring structure to this process.

After spending several weeks rebuilding and verifying the existing workforce on the whiteboard, I began designing a system to maintain both the physical crew boards and a new, digital version. This digital version not only provided management but also all departments with a clear view of the entire workforce.

My Role

I created and maintained a workforce tracking system that became the central source of crew information for the project.

While this workbook started as a tool for the Superintendents, it gradually expanded as different departments soon discovered they could use it without needing to call or visit another office.

Since I worked with this system every day, I continuously added new features whenever I found repetitive work, recurring questions, or information that could be presented more clearly or more easily.

Solution

Instead of simply creating another employee list, I designed a workbook around how people actually worked.

Moving someone between crews only required changing their name. Everything else – such as their shifts, classifications, training, crew counts, colors, and dashboards – was updated automatically.

Some key features included:

  • A centralized workforce tracker covering almost 1,000 employees across eight shifts and several work areas.
  • Search tools that provided information on employees, FM, GMs, and Superintendents rather than manually searching through dozens of crews.
  • Auto-populated cells of employee information using XLOOKUPs and structured tables.
  • A dynamic dashboard that showed crew sizes, shift distribution, and workforce balance, which helped support planning and recruitment decisions.
  • Training indicators alongside the crews so management could quickly see available certifications before moving employees.
  • Visual layouts and conditional formatting, making it so that reading so many crews is easier to understand at a glance.
  • Navigation buttons, locked worksheets, and hidden administration pages. This simplified the workbook for users, but it also helped protect critical formulas and prevent crew changes from being made without my knowledge.
  • Helper tools that allowed other departments to quickly match employee lists to FM, shifts, and their classifications, even when source data contained inconsistencies.


The workbook also supported our physical crew boards, which management used. After workforce changes were planned on the boards, I would then update both the company’s Master Workbook and my tracker so every department was working from the same information.

Instead of spending time searching for information, supervisors could focus on making decisions.
